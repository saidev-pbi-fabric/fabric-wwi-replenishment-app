Beyond the Canvas

What a weekend, one sample dataset, and a fully custom front end proved about what's actually possible on top of Fabric.

What's a Fabric Data App, in one line: a real web application (React) wired straight into a live Fabric semantic model. Same governed data your Power BI reports already use, but with a custom front end, and a write-back path Power BI's canvas doesn't offer natively.

From reading a risk signal to logging a decision

The app ranks every stock item by reorder risk, live off the model. Pick one, and the same screen lets you log a reorder action against it: quantity, supplier, who it's assigned to. Every change after that, status included, writes to an append-only audit trail. What changed, from what, to what, when, by whom.

See the risk, act on it, have the action logged and traceable. That loop is the whole pitch in one screen. A dashboard alone stops at "see it."

The real unlock

Code, not workarounds

This isn't a pitch to replace Power BI. Most reports never need any of this. But every Power BI build eventually hits the same wall: a visual the canvas can't render, an interaction it can't support, a rule that only fits inside an increasingly unreadable DAX measure. The usual answer is a workaround, or dropping the feature. A Fabric Data App removes that wall. The front end is React, the charts are Vega-Lite, not a fixed visual library. Anything a web app can do is on the table now, sitting on the exact same semantic model.

  • An adjustable concentration cutoff, live A slider re-draws the risk view's in-cutoff vs. past-cutoff split in real time. Not a fixed threshold baked into a measure. A control the viewer drives.
  • Two ranking views, one shared toggle Quantity-based and value-based risk rank, switchable everywhere at once, tie-breaking handled in code instead of a brittle DAX tiebreaker.
  • Write-back and an audit trail, natively Not an export-to-Excel-and-back loop. A form, a database write, a history log. Things a Power BI report simply can't do on its own. Access is authenticated, with no row-level restriction in this build.

Where has your team already hit that wall, and quietly shipped a workaround? Or just didn't ask?

Built entirely on the semantic model already in place. Nothing new stood up. If it's useful, ask for 20 minutes to walk through it live before deciding whether it's worth more.
